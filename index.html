About

I am a PhD candidate in Epidemiology and Biostatistics at the University of Hong Kong. I am interested in developing and applying causal inference methods for observational health data to improve causal validity, reduce bias, and support better clinical decision-making.

My PhD research is supervised by Prof. Ben Cowling. During my MSc in Statistics at the Southern University of Science and Technology, I was supervised by Prof. Guo-Liang Tian.

What I work on

Causal estimands, bias analysis, and correction

I study how selection, conditioning, and endpoint definitions change the causal quantity estimated in observational research, and how design or analysis can address the resulting bias.

Longitudinal observational health data

I use linked electronic health records, registries, cohorts, and hospital data to investigate disease progression, treatment context, and patient outcomes over time.

Vaccine and therapeutic evaluation

My applied work focuses on clinically meaningful evaluation of vaccines and treatments, with careful attention to target populations, time-to-event outcomes, and real-world study design.
